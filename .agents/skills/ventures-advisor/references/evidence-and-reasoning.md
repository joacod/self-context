# Ventures Evidence and Reasoning

This guide keeps initiative advice grounded in the user's actual project
records rather than in generic startup assumptions, activity volume, or a
model-generated business narrative.

## Build the smallest evidence set

Retrieve only what answers the current objective:

- the relevant Ventures record and its initiative lifecycle, current state,
  decisions, commitments, milestones, outcomes, and evolution;
- linked source records and provenance pointers;
- current, stale, reviewable, disputed, historical, and unknown state that can
  change the answer;
- Core constraints, values, preferences, or goals when the user asks for a
  choice or prioritization;
- Career evidence only for professional relevance, scope, impact, or stories;
- Learning evidence only for demonstrated understanding or a knowledge gap;
- Relationships evidence only for how the user knows a collaborator and their
  shared history; and
- Writing evidence only for communication or public-message fit.

A catalog line, tag, search score, or Advisor output is a retrieval aid, not
independent evidence. Inspect the linked page's assertion kind, status,
verification, freshness, and sources before relying on it.

Use language that exposes epistemic status:

- **Supported:** “The Ventures record states...”
- **Reported:** “The proposal/source records...”
- **Current but unconfirmed:** “The active page records..., with no explicit confirmation...”
- **Stale:** “The page records..., but its freshness deadline has passed...”
- **Reviewable:** “An inference suggests..., but this is not settled...”
- **Unknown:** “The vault does not establish...”
- **Derived:** “Given these constraints, I recommend...”

## Initiative and evidence states

Keep the following dimensions separate:

| Dimension | Meaning | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea / candidate / proposed / active / paused / completed / abandoned / superseded | The initiative's readable lifecycle | Adoption, success, viability, or future commitment |
| `active`, `review`, `archived`, and other shared page statuses | SelfContext page lifecycle | Initiative lifecycle |
| User-stated fact, source-derived fact, source record, inference, or derived synthesis | How a claim is supported | Verification from repetition or source presence |
| Discussion, proposal, agreement, decision, commitment, executed commitment | Strength of an interaction or action | A contract, deadline, or partner relationship |

Separate what an initiative demonstrates professionally (Career) from what the
person understands (Learning), how they relate to a collaborator (Relationships),
and what they generally prefer (Core). A project can be evidence for any of
those questions without owning the resulting claim.

Do not equate:

- repository activity with project activity or product delivery;
- a prototype with a shipped product;
- personal dogfooding with external adoption;
- one user's feedback with validated demand;
- interest with purchase intent;
- a stated outcome with a measured outcome;
- organizational access with employment;
- a public label with authority;
- a contribution with ownership; or
- a recommendation with a decision.

## Comparing initiatives

For a comparison or prioritization, first state the user's objective and
explicit constraints. Then compare only supported dimensions, such as:

- current lifecycle and actionable state;
- expected value as described by the user, without inventing market value;
- evidence of progress, outcomes, feedback, dogfooding, or adoption;
- effort, dependencies, commitments, collaborators, and reversibility when
  the record supports them;
- fit with Core constraints and preferences;
- Career, Learning, or relationship implications when explicitly relevant; and
- unknowns that could materially change the choice.

Do not fill an absent dimension with a guess. “No evidence of adoption” is not
necessarily “no adoption”; report the unknown. “No recorded commitment” is not
necessarily “no commitment” when the record is incomplete.

A useful comparison names alternatives and trade-offs, not just a winner. Make
conditional recommendations and state what evidence would change them. Do not
turn prioritization advice into a durable goal or project decision without an
explicit user action.

## Collaborators and organizations

Use project records for the collaborator's role in the initiative only when
that role is evidenced. A proposal from an organization is not an engagement;
working together is not automatically a partnership; a title or access level is
not employment, compensation, equity, ownership, or contractual authority.
Relationships owns how the user knows the person and their shared history.
Never infer motives, reliability, intent, sensitive traits, or organizational
characteristics.

If the question is “how do I know this person?”, route to Relationships. If it
is “what did this initiative demonstrate professionally?”, route to Career. If
it is “what did I learn while building it?”, route to Learning. Ventures can
supply linked initiative context to those answers without duplicating their
facts.

## Dogfooding, adoption, and outcomes

Describe personal use as personal dogfooding. Describe external use only when
there is explicit evidence of external users or adoption, and keep the scope
and source visible. Feedback, interest, an invitation, a download, or a
conversation may be useful evidence, but none independently proves demand,
purchase intent, revenue, product-market fit, or business viability.

Preserve milestones and outcomes as dated evidence. A milestone can show that a
stated event occurred; it does not automatically establish project success. A
failed experiment, abandonment, pause, or poor outcome remains useful history
when it explains later decisions.

## Freshness and uncertainty

Treat `stale_after` as a review deadline, not a truth value. Historical evidence
can remain useful after it expires. If a stale current-state claim controls a
recommendation, give the supported historical or conditional answer and ask one
bounded freshness question. `stale_after: null` means no automated deadline,
not proof that a dynamic claim is current.

Keep unknowns visible. Do not resolve contradictions by choosing the newest,
most detailed, or most enthusiastic account. Preserve both accounts and state
the smallest human decision or evidence request needed.
