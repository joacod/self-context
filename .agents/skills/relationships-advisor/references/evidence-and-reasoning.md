# Relationships Evidence and Reasoning

This guide keeps relationship answers centered on the user's connection with a
person and prevents a useful continuity tool from becoming a third-party
profile or social surveillance system.

## Build the smallest evidence set

Retrieve only what answers the current objective:

- the named relationship page and its relevant shared-context sections;
- source records for the specific conversation, event, or commitment;
- selected review observations, stale entries, or contradictions that affect
  the answer;
- Career, Ventures, Writing, Learning, Media / Taste, or `core/` pages only
  when their owning evidence is necessary; and
- derived material only as labeled preparation or recommendation, never as
  independent relationship evidence.

For each candidate page, ask:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Is this about the user's relationship? | It prevents unrelated third-party facts from becoming durable context. |
| Who supplied or reported it? | A user recollection, another person's statement, and an external source have different provenance. |
| What does the evidence actually show? | An interaction does not prove a motive, trait, or relationship strength. |
| Is the context current and relevant to this interaction? | Old plans and stale threads should not be presented as active. |
| Is it reviewable or contradicted? | Provisional evidence must remain conditional. |
| Is the detail necessary to answer the question? | Least-context retrieval reduces privacy exposure. |

Use language such as:

- **Supported:** “The relationship page records...”
- **Reported:** “The user recalls that Alice said...”
- **Observed:** “The last two planned meetings were cancelled.”
- **Provisional:** “A reviewable observation suggests..., but the vault does not establish...”
- **Derived:** “For the upcoming conversation, I recommend...”
- **Unknown:** “The relationship context does not say...”

## Commitments and open loops

Look for an actor, action, evidence, status, and time or context. Separate:

- a promise the user made;
- a promise another person reportedly made;
- a mutual agreement;
- a tentative idea that the user still wants to remember; and
- a resolved, declined, or obsolete thread.

Do not treat “we should do that sometime” as a task unless the user retained it
as a relationship open loop. Do not manufacture due dates, priority, reminders,
or completion status from ordinary conversation.

## Relationship evolution

Use dated evidence and explicit descriptions. A change from coworker to former
coworker may be supported by a role change; it does not establish friendship or
closeness unless the user says so. Preserve older descriptions when they explain
shared history. A new interaction is not automatically evidence that the
relationship changed.

## Privacy and third-party boundaries

Behavioral evidence can be useful without becoming a judgment. Say that two
meetings were cancelled rather than diagnosing unreliability. Do not infer
medical, mental-health, sexual, religious, political, ethnic, criminal,
financial, personality, or motivational attributes. Do not use public profiles or
social-media availability as a reason to create a record.

If a sensitive detail was explicitly supplied and is genuinely necessary for a
narrow user-stated purpose, minimize it, identify the provenance, avoid
repeating it in a broad answer, and keep the user's ability to remove it
visible. A request to delete or redact overrides the usefulness of an old source;
do not resurrect the detail.

## Cross-vertical ownership

Career may own a professional role or its professional outcome. Ventures owns
initiative lifecycle and project decisions. Writing may own a message draft or
communication pattern. Learning may own what the user understood. Media / Taste
may own a reaction to a film. Relationships may link those pages when the shared
relationship is the point, but it must not duplicate the other claim.

## Insufficient evidence

When the evidence is thin:

1. answer the supported relationship fact or shared event;
2. label what is reported, inferred, stale, or unknown;
3. avoid a personality or closeness conclusion; and
4. ask one bounded question only if it would materially change the requested
   interaction guidance.

A useful result can be “No meaningful relationship update” when the interaction
is incidental, already represented, or not worth retaining.
