# Writing Evidence and Reasoning

This guide keeps Writing advice grounded in observable evidence without turning
the vault into a static personality profile.

## Build the Smallest Evidence Set

For a personalized request, retrieve only what helps answer the current
objective:

- relevant Writing concepts and their supporting source records;
- mode, language, audience, and dated scope;
- explicit user preferences or corrections;
- meaningful human revisions of assisted drafts;
- relevant core, career, project, belief, or knowledge pages owned elsewhere;
- unresolved contradictions and review items; and
- derived analyses only as labeled recommendations, never as independent
  evidence.

Inspect each candidate pattern for:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Who authored the source? | Generated language must not feed back as authentic voice. |
| What is the assertion kind? | User statement, source record, inference, and synthesis have different trust. |
| What is the scope? | A message, technical article, opinion essay, and Spanish text may be different modes. |
| How independent is the evidence? | Repeated phrases in one source are not multiple observations. |
| When was it observed? | Writing can evolve; older evidence is not automatically current or wrong. |
| Is there a contradiction? | Preserve mixed evidence instead of silently choosing. |
| Does it answer the objective? | Relevant evidence is better than a comprehensive style profile. |

## Evidence Priority

Use this qualitative order while preserving the original assertion kinds:

1. An explicit user preference, correction, or adoption, within its stated
   scope.
2. Genuine user-authored material with known provenance.
3. Meaningful human changes to an AI-assisted draft, analyzed as a delta.
4. A scoped inference supported by diverse sources and still marked as such.
5. Generated drafts, summaries, critiques, and prior derived advice, which may
   support the current task but cannot independently establish a user pattern.

An explicit preference outranks a weak behavioral inference, but it does not
automatically apply to modes or audiences the user did not name.

## Pattern, Belief, and Recommendation

Keep these forms separate:

- **Writing pattern:** "Technical essays often move from a concrete example to
  the underlying abstraction."
- **User preference:** "For public technical essays, I prefer problem-first
  openings." This is user-stated and scoped.
- **Belief or opinion:** "AI tools reduce the value of memorizing syntax." This
  belongs to the appropriate domain, not Writing.
- **Recommendation:** "For this audience, define the term before the example."
  This is Advisor reasoning, not durable user context.

Do not infer that a person believes a claim because it would fit their style.
Do not treat a repeated topic as a writing preference or a polished draft as a
personal fact.

## Confidence Without Fake Precision

Explain confidence through evidence state and rationale:

- **Candidate:** narrow or single-source signal that needs more evidence.
- **Emerging:** repeated or diverse support with remaining uncertainty.
- **Established:** consistent independent support for a clear scope.
- **Explicit preference:** directly stated or confirmed by the user.

Use source quality, independence, context diversity, consistency, and time. Do
not count every new ingestion as a confidence change, and do not use model
certainty or source repetition as verification. A well-established pattern can
remain unchanged after another consistent source.

## Modes and Evolution

Prefer a scoped statement when evidence varies:

```text
In technical/explanatory writing, examples often precede abstraction. In short
messages, the user usually states the action directly with little background.
Evidence for these modes should not be averaged into one universal voice.
```

When dates show a genuine change, describe the earlier and later periods and
preserve both source sets. When the difference may be a mode or exception, say
so and keep the observation reviewable. Do not assume that recent writing is
better; it is simply more recent evidence.

## Reader Model

Ground reader analysis in observable choices:

- assumed technical knowledge;
- where terminology receives explanation;
- amount of background and context;
- use and placement of examples;
- anticipated objections and tradeoffs;
- relevance before detail;
- information compression and purposeful repetition; and
- precision versus accessibility for the named audience.

Do not infer the identity, intelligence, or psychology of the reader or author.

## Revisions

For an AI draft and human revision, preserve both sides when useful and describe
only the meaningful delta:

- lexical replacements or deleted generic phrasing;
- shorter or longer constructions;
- added or removed context;
- reordered paragraphs or arguments;
- changed examples, claims, openings, or conclusions; and
- deliberate tone or rhythm changes.

One edit is a candidate signal. Repeated independent revisions or an explicit
user explanation may justify a scoped refinement. The original AI draft remains
generated and cannot validate its own style.

## Insufficient Evidence

When the evidence is thin:

1. Answer the portion supported by the available sources.
2. Name what is unknown, unverified, stale, or contradictory.
3. Offer a bounded next step, such as asking for one authored example or the
   intended mode.

Do not compensate with generic confidence, adjectives, psychoanalysis, or
invented personal history.
