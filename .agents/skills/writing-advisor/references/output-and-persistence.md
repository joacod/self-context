# Writing Outputs and Persistence

## Idea and Brainstorming Outputs

Start with thought development when the user provides a rough idea. A useful
response may include:

- the likely objective and target reader;
- a tentative thesis or central tension;
- assumptions, missing reasoning, and counterarguments;
- possible angles, examples, structures, titles, or endings; and
- connections to relevant SelfContext evidence, clearly labeled as context.

Do not jump to polished prose when the idea has not been worked through. Do not
force historical Writing patterns when a new angle would better serve the
reader.

## Drafting Outputs

Before drafting, establish the useful constraints: purpose, reader, mode,
length, language, source evidence, and what should remain unchanged. Use generic
writing expertise for clarity, structure, argument, pacing, grammar, and
reader fit. Use personal Writing context only as scoped evidence about how the
user communicates.

Generated drafts should be labeled as drafts or suggestions. They must not be
described as authentic user writing, used as primary evidence, or written back
into the Writing profile automatically.

## Editing Outputs

For user-authored input:

1. Identify what already works.
2. Suggest changes only where the benefit is meaningful.
3. Preserve intentional imperfections, personality, strong positions, fragments,
   unusual pacing, and context-specific language.
4. Explain changes in terms of reader comprehension, reasoning, precision,
   rhythm, structure, redundancy, or impact.
5. Offer a light-touch alternative when a full rewrite would erase voice.

The Advisor may conclude "keep this". Smoothness is not the same as quality,
and generic fluency is not the default objective.

## Reader Analysis Outputs

Review from the intended reader's perspective, then reconcile the result with the
user's preferences and mode. Call out missing context, reasoning jumps,
repetition, overexplaining, unsupported claims, weak examples, skeptical
objections, openings that do not earn attention, and conclusions that merely
repeat the article. Do not turn each generic best practice into a personal
preference.

## Evidence and Cross-Vertical Context

When a draft uses personal context:

- cite or name the relevant Writing observation and its scope;
- retrieve career, project, belief, knowledge, and core context from their owning
  areas;
- do not copy those pages into Writing or present a recommendation as a fact;
- preserve uncertainty and contradictions that affect the draft; and
- never infer an opinion merely because it sounds compatible with the user's
  style.

For a professional bio or cover letter, Career evidence remains career evidence
and Writing context supplies communication fit. For a technical explanation,
technical knowledge remains in its owning context and Writing supplies the mode,
reader, and reasoning guidance.

## Persistence Decision

Ordinary responses remain ephemeral:

- a rough outline, brainstorm, draft, edit, critique, or reader analysis does
  not update the Writing profile;
- a source may be retained when the user supplies it or asks SelfContext to
  preserve it;
- a meaningful human revision may be analyzed as future evidence through the
  SelfContext Writing workflow; and
- a substantial reusable analysis may be stored under `vault/derived/` as a
  linked `derived_synthesis` page.

Before any vault mutation, SelfContext creates the local pre-write backup. A
derived page is not a source for a new Writing observation and cannot silently
change a goal, belief, career fact, or preference.

## Compact Response Shape

Use only what fits the request:

```markdown
## Intent and reader

...

## Evidence and choices

...

## Options, draft, or edits

...

## What changed and what stayed

...

## Uncertainty

...
```

Keep generated text, evidence, inference, and recommendation visibly distinct.
