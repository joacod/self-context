# Learning Evidence and Reasoning

This guide keeps Learning answers useful without turning exposure, model
confidence, or generated explanations into a false inventory of competence.

## Build the smallest evidence set

Retrieve only the pages needed for the question:

- relevant `learning/` concepts and their supporting source records;
- knowledge state, scope, dates, progression, gaps, corrections, and
  prerequisite links in those pages;
- selected `review/observations/` pages tagged `learning` when they explain
  uncertainty or a possible gap;
- Career or Ventures project pages, and Writing pages, only when they provide
  evidence owned there;
  and
- derived material only as labeled analysis, never as independent evidence.

For each page, inspect:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What is the assertion kind? | A user statement, source record, inference, and synthesis do not have the same evidentiary status. |
| What does the source actually show? | A project or course may show activity without proving the claimed understanding. |
| What is the scope? | A person may demonstrate a concept in one task without knowing every adjacent use. |
| Is it current? | Dates, `stale_after`, and explicit outdated context affect whether the state is useful now. |
| Is it provisional or contradicted? | Review items and competing models should produce conditional answers, not silent selection. |
| Does it answer this question? | A small relevant set is safer than a biography of everything encountered. |

Use the following language:

- **Supported:** “The Learning context records...” or “This exercise supports...”
- **Scoped:** “The evidence demonstrates this in...”
- **Provisional:** “A reviewable observation suggests...”
- **Unknown:** “The vault does not establish...”
- **Derived:** “For this explanation, I recommend...”

An active page with `verified: null` can still be useful as unconfirmed
user-stated or source-supported evidence when its scope and freshness fit. A
review page or agent inference cannot support a confident claim by itself. An
expired current-sensitive page is historical until confirmed.

## Distinguish exposure, understanding, and demonstration

Use the evidence, not a label alone:

- **Exposure:** a resource, course, conversation, or mention shows that the
  topic was encountered. Do not call this understanding.
- **Intentional learning:** the user says they are working on the topic or the
  evidence shows a sustained, meaningful effort. This describes activity and
  direction, not competence.
- **Partial understanding:** the user or evidence identifies a coherent part
  that works and a concrete boundary that does not.
- **Understanding:** the user explicitly reports a coherent model within a
  named scope, or the evidence supports that statement without broadening it.
- **Demonstrated knowledge:** an explanation, exercise, or project shows the
  person can apply or explain a concept in a defined situation. State the task,
  complexity, and limitations.
- **Uncertain or outdated:** preserve the reason for uncertainty or the event
  that made prior knowledge no longer current. Time alone is not evidence of
  forgetting.

Do not turn multiple agent summaries into independent evidence. Repeated
questions can identify a gap only when they are meaningful and the gap is
likely to matter again; they do not prove inability.

## Gaps, misconceptions, and mental models

Call a gap durable when it is explicitly important, blocks a likely future task,
recurs across meaningful attempts, or is a user-confirmed observation. Phrase it
as a boundary, for example: “Can explain reconciliation, but the exact
concurrent-rendering scheduling behavior remains unclear.”

For a misconception:

1. identify the earlier model and its date or source;
2. identify the correction and why it is supported;
3. state whether the correction is user-stated, source-derived, or still an
   agent inference; and
4. preserve what remains uncertain or context-dependent.

A mental model is personal only when the user states it or evidence shows that
they use it to reason. Do not substitute a generic teaching explanation for the
person's model.

## Explaining from known context

When asked to explain a new concept:

1. retrieve supported concepts that are genuinely relevant prerequisites;
2. exclude stale, review-only, or merely encountered concepts from being
   presented as settled foundations;
3. state the bridge from the known concept to the new one;
4. introduce only the minimum new vocabulary needed;
5. mark where the explanation depends on generic knowledge rather than the
   vault; and
6. invite correction if the assumed prerequisite is wrong, without writing a
   new fact automatically.

A prerequisite relationship may be personal and useful without being a
universal curriculum. If the vault has no suitable foundation, say what is
missing and give a generic short bridge rather than pretending the person knows
it.

## Progression and cross-vertical evidence

For “how has my understanding changed?” use dated evidence entries, corrections,
and state history. Preserve earlier periods and distinguish a mode change from
actual learning when the evidence permits both interpretations. A later source
is not automatically more reliable or more complete.

Career owns professional relevance and outcomes; Ventures owns initiative
lifecycle and project decisions; Writing owns authored communication patterns
and the source record for an article. They can support a Learning claim through
links, but they are not duplicated into `learning/` and cannot be used to infer
understanding beyond what they show.

When evidence is insufficient, answer the supported portion, name the missing
piece, and ask at most one bounded follow-up. Do not compensate with generic
confidence or a numerical score.
